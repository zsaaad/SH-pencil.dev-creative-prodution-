## Cross-cutting patterns

- **Day-N proof is harder than launch-day proof.** Square's PJ's Coffee was launch-day proof and Toast's Hungry Howie's was earned-cycle proof; Square's The Hat was Day-4-of-NRA proof. The pattern that's emerging is that you ship the first installed-customer name on launch day, then ship a multi-unit chain announcement as a show-floor closer. Two named-customer wins, one event window. SEA POS rivals tend to ship one named-customer per cycle, then go dark.
- **Sales-lift numbers beat AI claims.** Toast IQ Grow's 8% average sales lift and 40% top-quartile lift do more selling than any "AI marketing agent" headline. SumUp's "no monthly fees" and HitPay's published transaction rates do more selling than "best-in-class pricing." The brands quoting specific operator-outcome numbers in their headlines are pulling away from the brands quoting features.
- **Public divestiture as positioning.** Lightspeed's Upserve sale was not just balance-sheet hygiene — it was the structural setup for the FY27 narrative. Selling something publicly is more credible than pretending the failed bet didn't happen. SEA POS rivals that have quietly retired products (BigPOS variants, Mobi POS modules, Slurp! product lines) should consider naming the prune as a brand move.
- **Marketing-success-manager as the AI guardrail.** Toast bundling a human Marketing Success Manager into IQ Grow is the model — AI alone doesn't sell; AI + a named human strategist sells. Foodics' "Forward Together" personalizes the brand against macro pressure the same way. The AI-first brands that don't pair the agent with a human face are losing the trust corridor.
- **Vertical-specific content is unclaimed in SEA.** Qashier shipped two long-form Malaysia salon guides in a single week. No other SEA POS is doing vertical-specific content with that cadence. Beauty, F&B (broken into kopitiam, mamak, hawker, dim sum, halal kitchen), retail (broken into pasar stall, kiosk, mini-market), wellness — each is an open content lane.
- **The MDR conversation is the next SEA POS wedge.** 77% of Malaysian SMEs cite high merchant discount rates as a barrier to QR adoption. No POS has built creative around lowering MDR yet. The first one wins both the rail-borrow story and the friction story in one ad.
