## Cross-cutting patterns

- **Brand-platform films are back, and Toast set the pace.** "Built For Busy" reframes "busy" from overwhelm to badge of honor across OLV, radio, social, OOH, and influencer cuts. The pivot is structural — most POS ads sell features; brand-platform films sell identity ("you are the busy one and we built this for you"). The first SEA POS to ship a Toast-caliber brand platform — not a feature spot — wins the operator's emotional surface for the year.
- **Living-billboard OOH is the cheap, copyable physical activation.** NYC "Windows to Success" turns partner restaurant storefronts into framed performance spaces — no ad-network media spend, no out-of-home agency, just custom graphics on existing windows. SEA read: KL Bukit Bintang shophouses, Bangkok Sukhumvit street-level restaurants, Manila BGC F&B counters — all open for the same treatment. Cost is print and install, not media.
- **Pilot lift numbers beat product feature claims.** Toast IQ Grow's "8% average, 40% top quartile, Matoi 3x ROI in 60 days" trio is the credibility set every POS AI claim should benchmark against. Square Managerbot still hasn't published a lift number; Lightspeed AI still hasn't. The first POS in market with a published pilot-tier number plus a named operator's logo wins the AI claim category.
- **Compliance as a sales hook, not a sales burden.** Qashier shipped BCRS auto-handling as a feature claim the morning of the April 1 deadline. The LHDN e-invoice mandate, TRD digital tax stamp, BIR CAS — every SEA market has a regulation moment. Whoever ships the "we already handle this" creative on the morning of the deadline owns the compliance-anxious operator.
- **Network effects as the new POS moat.** Neighborhoods on Cash App ($320M GPV, up 190%) shows the social-graph layer on top of POS is structurally different from a loyalty program. The "regulars drive 6x more revenue" stat from Square's Local Economy Report is the most quotable POS-marketing number this cycle and translates into any SEA language. The structural play (regulars-as-network) translates; the Cash-App-specific stat (6x) is directly liftable.
- **Vertical-software wedges are the new positioning surface.** Clover Reserve for fine dining, Tabit underneath. Toast IQ Grow for restaurant marketing. Lightspeed Reservations / Tempo / Tasks for restaurant ops. SEA POS competing on horizontal "all-in-one" is now competing against vertical AI stacks. The wedge response is to pick one SEA-specific vertical (mamak, kopitiam, halal QSR, hawker, salon, dive shop) and ship a dedicated vertical claim around it.
- **Pay-later inside the existing QR, no redirect.** HitPay's GrabPay PayLater embedded QR removes the off-site credit step. SEA pay-later (Atome, ShopBack PayLater, Grab PayLater) bundled inside the Beep QR without redirect is the structurally identical move available to StoreHub.
- **SaaS-style raw founder/operator video is the format SEA POS still isn't shipping.** Across spy-tool databases in 2026, TikTok-style raw founder videos (45–90 seconds, original audio, no studio production) outperform polished creative by 30–40% in CTR. Toast's NYC influencer partnerships (@tastebywill, @hungryartistny, Shawn Walchef) lean into this register. SEA POS has the operators (StoreHub merchants in Pulau Redang, Tioman, BGC, Sukhumvit) but isn't shipping the raw cuts.

The same move is available in SEA for words like "sibuk" (busy), "penat" (tired), "lambat" (slow). The structural lesson: the creative agency's brief was a word, not a feature. https://adage.com/creativity/work/aa-toast-built-for-busy/
- **NYC "Windows to Success" — earned-media OOH via partner storefronts.** No billboard buy, no transit-card buy — just custom window graphics on partner restaurant windows that turn storefronts into performance stages. Available in any SEA F&B-dense district at print-and-install cost. https://pos.toasttab.com/news/toast-built-for-busy-nyc-windows-to-success
- **Pencil × Coty — embedded creative-OS as a multi-brand credibility card.** Already in the May 22 / June 1 reports; still the strongest enterprise validation card in the Pencil category. Use the stat lines ("10M ads, $4B in spend, world's #1 Gen AI marketing platform") verbatim in any SEA mid-market outreach. https://www.coty.com/news/coty-partners-with-pencil-to-build-end-to-end-gen-ai-content-system
- **Square × Eric Wareheim — comedic celebrity as the counter-register to earnest brand films.** Wareheim's deadpan voiceover over LA restaurateur B-roll is the opposite register of Toast "Built For Busy" earnest reframe. SEA equivalents — Harith Iskander in Malaysia, Joe Devilla in the Philippines, Note Udom in Thailand — open the same comedic register at lower cost than a global celebrity. https://block.xyz/news/square
- **TikTok founder-led raw video — the 45–90-second format converting for SaaS.** Raw founder video outperforms polished creative by 30–40% CTR in 2026. The Loom, Vercel, Supabase, Linear ad cuts are the format reference. SEA POS founders shipping a 60-second iPhone video of "this is what your lunch rush looks like on Beep" beats any studio cut at 10x lower production cost. https://usevisuals.com/blog/b2b-saas-tiktok-strategy-2026 and https://adlibrary.com/posts/best-saas-ads-examples-2026

## Creative briefs for Pencil.dev

Five concepts derived from this run. None duplicates briefs from `2026-06-01-ad-inspiration.md`.

### Brief 1 — "Built For Sibuk" — SEA brand-platform mirror of Toast "Built For Busy"

- **Audience:** SEA F&B operators — kopitiam owners, mamak operators, hawker stall vendors, kedai runcit shopkeepers — who see "sibuk" (busy) as the daily reality, not a brand promise.
- **Format:** Multi-asset brand-platform campaign — 60-second hero film + 15-second companion + Bahasa/English vertical cuts for Reels + a physical KL/Penang/Ipoh shophouse "Windows to Success" OOH activation. Hero film opens on a roti canai master calmly flipping dough in the middle of a packed mamak rush, camera pulls back to street POV through the open shopfront, narration: "Sibuk bermakna semua kerja sedang bekerja. Sibuk adalah kejayaan." Companion :15 intercuts methodical nasi lemak plating with a rapid-fire kitchen-printer to-do list flashing in Bahasa.
- **Hook:** "Sibuk bermakna semua kerja sedang bekerja. Sibuk adalah kejayaan." ("Busy means all your work is working. Busy is success.")
- **Visual direction:** Real Malaysian mamak / kopitiam / hawker B-roll. Slow calm hands in the middle of chaos. Camera pulls back through the open shopfront — same structural rhyme Toast used in NYC. Bahasa Malaysia narration with English subtitles for the cross-market cuts. StoreHub orange (#ff9419) on black (#2f2922), Barlow + Open Sans throughout. End frame: "Dibina untuk yang sibuk." ("Built for the busy.")
- **CTA:** "Sertai 10,000+ yang sibuk. Cuba StoreHub percuma 14 hari."
- **Differentiator vs. Toast "Built For Busy":** Toast owns "Busy" in English. StoreHub can own "Sibuk" in Bahasa, "งานยุ่ง" in Thai, "abala" in Tagalog — three brand-platform variants for the same emotional reframe across MY/TH/PH. Cost a fraction of the Toast/Arnold spend; available immediately; structurally identical creative move with three-market localization Toast can't easily replicate.

### Brief 2 — "Regulars Drive 6x More Revenue" — Beep Cashback network-effect ad

- **Audience:** SEA F&B operators running Beep / StoreHub who think of loyalty as a punch card rather than as the operator's biggest revenue lever.
- **Format:** A 30-second hero film + 4-slide LinkedIn carousel + Meta single-image variant. Open on the stat in bold ("6x") against StoreHub orange. Cut to operator-level B-roll — same customer walking in three times, four times, five times across the same week. Closing card: "10 regulars > 100 walk-ins. Beep Cashback turns customers into regulars."
- **Hook:** "Regulars drive 6x more revenue. Beep Cashback turns customers into regulars."
- **Visual direction:** Hard-stat open (large "6x" on orange), repeat-customer sequence (same face, three time-stamps), Beep Cashback app UI cut-ins showing the points-earn + reorder flow. End frame on the Membership List Page (consolidated cashback across brands) as the proof point that Beep is a network, not a single-brand loyalty card.
- **CTA:** "Hidupkan pelanggan tetap. Cuba Beep Cashback hari ini."
- **Differentiator vs. Square Neighborhoods:** Square has the stat; StoreHub can localize it. The "6x" number works in any market and any language. The Membership List Page is structurally the same play as Cash App's follow-network — multi-brand membership in one consolidated view. Pair the Square-published stat with the StoreHub-shipped feature and the credibility card is built.

### Brief 3 — "LHDN E-invois? Sudah Selesai." — compliance-as-feature ad on regulatory-deadline morning

- **Audience:** Malaysian SMEs facing the next LHDN e-invoice mandate phase, especially F&B and retail operators who don't have a finance team and are anxious about the compliance step.
- **Format:** A 20-second vertical hook film + 4-slide LinkedIn carousel + static MY-Bahasa Meta image. Open on a worried-looking operator scrolling a news article about the LHDN e-invoice deadline. Cut to the same operator's POS receipt printer auto-printing a full receipt with the e-invoice QR code already on it. Closing card: "Beep handle e-invois automatik. Anda jaga kedai."
- **Hook:** "LHDN e-invois? Sudah selesai. Beep handle automatik."
- **Visual direction:** Worried-operator-scrolling-news cold open (high-anxiety register), then hard-cut to calm cashier-counter B-roll with the receipt printer doing its work, QR code close-up. End frame on the LHDN-compliance auto-print as the headline feature. StoreHub orange on black, Barlow + Open Sans.
- **CTA:** "Cuba Beep. Lupakan e-invois."
- **Differentiator vs. Qashier BCRS play:** Qashier ran the structural play in Singapore on April 1. StoreHub runs the identical play in Malaysia on the next LHDN deadline morning. Whoever ships the "we already handle this" creative on deadline day owns the compliance-anxious operator for the next 6 months. The auto-print is already in the Beep changelog — this brief turns the feature into the creative.

### Brief 4 — "60 Seconds of Beep. Real Lunch Rush." — raw founder-style screen-recording cut

- **Audience:** SEA operator who has scrolled past enough UGC competitor ads and wants to see the product working, not a talking head.
- **Format:** A 60-second pure screen-recording + counter-cam ad. No voiceover artist, no actor, no stock footage. Real Beep / StoreHub tablet capture of a real Malaysian or Thai lunch service, intercut with one over-the-shoulder counter cam shot of the operator's hand. Captions in plain text: "12:14 — order in," "12:14 — table 4 modifications," "12:15 — kitchen ticket auto-routes to halal station," "12:17 — DuitNow QR scan," "12:18 — receipt printed, table cleared." Subtle ambient sound (no music) — just the product doing the thing.
- **Hook:** "60 seconds of Beep. One real lunch rush."
- **Visual direction:** Screen-recorded UI throughout + single counter-cam cut. Captions in Open Sans, English with Bahasa subtitle option. End frame plain: "This is what your lunch rush looks like on Beep." StoreHub orange logo bottom-right only.
- **CTA:** "Lihat satu lagi minit. Cuba Beep percuma 14 hari."
- **Differentiator vs. UGC-style competitor ads:** UGC dominates Meta in 2026. A pure screen-recording cut is the format Loom, Vercel, Supabase, and Linear are using to bypass "tell me about your product" skepticism. No SEA POS is running this format. Available immediately, low production cost, structurally differentiated from every competitor's first-person testimonial.

### Brief 5 — "Berjaya Hotels Powered by StoreHub" — SEA hospitality logo + handheld co-launch (Clover/Toast hospitality mirror)

- **Audience:** SEA multi-property hospitality groups — Berjaya Hotels & Resorts, Sunway Resort, Sime Darby Property F&B, Tune Hotels — who run multi-concept F&B across pool deck, lobby restaurant, room service, banquet, retail. Plus the SEA mid-market F&B chains watching these logos.
- **Format:** A two-beat campaign mirroring the Toast Go 3 / Preferred Hotels and Clover Reserve / Tabit moves. Beat 1: a 45-second hardware + multi-concept hospitality film featuring a single named SEA hospitality logo (Berjaya, Sunway, Tune, or Centara). Pool deck to lobby restaurant to room service to grab 'n go on one handheld. Beat 2 (one week later): a 20-second handheld-spec close-up — battery hours, drop-tolerance, water-resistance, payment-method count.
- **Hook (beat 1):** "From poolside to room service. {Hospitality logo}, powered by StoreHub."
- **Hook (beat 2):** "Built for the SEA hospitality floor. 24 hours of battery. Every payment method. One handheld."
- **Visual direction:** Hospitality-property B-roll (pool decks, lobby restaurants, banquet floors), handheld in-hand on staff aprons, multi-concept icon overlays. StoreHub orange-on-black brand intersected with the hospitality partner's brand stack.
- **CTA (beat 1):** "Read the {hospitality logo} story."
- **CTA (beat 2):** "Try the handheld. 14 days free."
- **Differentiator vs. Toast Preferred Hotels / Clover Reserve / Tabit:** Toast owns the Western luxury hospitality logo (Preferred Hotels), Clover is buying the fine-dining logo (Tabit). The SEA luxury hospitality logo is still uncontested. Berjaya, Sunway, Centara, Banyan Tree, Dusit, Shangri-La SEA — whichever group signs first becomes the SEA equivalent of Toast's Preferred Hotels Alliance card. The two-beat sequencing (Toast did Go 3 → Preferred; Clover is doing Reserve → SMCC Japan) is the template.
