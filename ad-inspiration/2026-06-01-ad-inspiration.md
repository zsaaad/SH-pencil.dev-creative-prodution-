# Competitor Ad Inspiration — 2026-06-01

## TL;DR

- Square's Managerbot opened broader beta on April 28 and is on track for U.S. general availability in June — making it the first POS-embedded AI agent that is proactive rather than reactive. Block Q1 2026 framed it as the clearest single proof point for Jack Dorsey's AI bet. This is structurally different from Toast IQ Grow (which markets AI marketing) — Managerbot markets AI management (inventory forecasting, shift scheduling, automated marketing) at $0 incremental cost. The category fault-line has shifted from "do you have AI marketing?" to "does your AI do the manager's job?"
- Coty × Pencil is the largest Gen-AI marketing partnership in beauty so far this year (May 4). A dedicated embedded Pencil team starts at Coty July 1 across CoverGirl, Rimmel, Sally Hansen, Max Factor — multi-brand, multi-market, end-to-end. The press surface ("the world's #1 generative AI marketing platform," "10M+ ads since 2018," "$4BN+ in media spend") gives Pencil the cleanest enterprise validation card in the category. This is the credibility anchor any Pencil ad to mid-market SEA can lean on without invention.
- Toast made two moves in the same week (May 4–5) that re-shape the hospitality narrative: the Preferred Hotels & Resorts Alliance partnership (625 hotels, 80 countries, US/UK/IE/CA) and the global launch of Toast Go 3 in those same four markets a week earlier (April 28). Hospitality + handheld hardware is now a unified plot, and Toast Go 3's 24-hour battery / 6.52-inch screen / IP65 / 16% lighter spec sheet is the hero asset Square has nothing equivalent to ship.
- Lightspeed shipped Lightspeed AI on January 9 (conversational assistants inside Retail + Restaurant) and added a second AI/payments/wholesale wave on May 7 alongside the Bhawna Singh CTO appointment. The May 7 release notes were under-covered relative to the earnings sell-off — the actual operating story is "AI inside the workflow, on cadence, every quarter," not "stock dropped 7%."
- StoreHub's competitive lane on home turf got more crowded on May 28: EasyStore signed a four-way MoU with SUNMI Malaysia, Ninja Van Malaysia, and Alliance Bank to support 1,000+ SMEs with integrated payments + logistics + financing + commerce. This is the first SEA POS-adjacent player to publicly bundle a banking partner into an unified-commerce alliance — a structural threat to any "all-in-one POS" pitch that doesn't include a credit line.

## Cross-cutting patterns

- **Proactive AI is the new claim — reactive AI is now table-stakes.** Square's Managerbot moves the goalposts from "AI you talk to" (Lightspeed AI, Toast Smart AI Assistant) to "AI that talks to you." Every POS in the next cycle will claim some flavor of proactive. The winning brands will be the ones quoting the merchant-side approval rate ("9 out of 10 Managerbot actions approved by sellers") rather than the AI-side action count. Trust-via-approval is the new positioning surface.
- **Two-beat product narratives beat one-beat announcements.** Toast paired Toast Go 3 (April 28) with Preferred Hotels (May 4) inside a single week. Lightspeed paired Lightspeed AI (Jan 9) with the May 7 wave + CTO appointment. Block paired Q1 earnings with Managerbot beta. The brands sequencing two related beats inside a week are getting more coverage than the ones publishing single-press-release moments — the second beat re-amplifies the first.
- **Hardware spec sheets are coming back into POS creative.** Toast Go 3's "24-hour battery, IP65, 5-foot drop, 16% lighter, 6.52-inch screen" is the most concrete handheld spec sheet from any POS this cycle. HitPay POS Max followed with the Sunmi V3 Mix spec list a few weeks later. The hardware-claim era is back after a 2024–25 stretch of pure-software claims, and SEA POS without a hardware story risks looking like a thin app on someone else's terminal.
- **The credit-line wedge is opening.** EasyStore's MoU bundled Alliance Bank into the unified-commerce alliance — the first SEA POS-adjacent player to make "your POS earns you bank credit" the headline. Toast Capital, Square Loans, Shopify Capital have all run this play in the U.S., but no SEA POS has surfaced it as a brand-level claim. The first SEA POS to ship a working-capital line wired into the POS surface wins the cash-flow conversation operators actually care about.
- **Embedded enterprise deals are the credibility shortcut.** Coty × Pencil is the strongest example this cycle — a five-brand multi-market embed beats a single-customer testimonial as a credibility card. Toast's Preferred Hotels Alliance is the structurally identical move from the POS side. SEA read: a single embedded SEA-multi-brand partner (a Berjaya Food, a Texas Chicken franchise group, a Sushi Tei regional operator, a Secret Recipe chain) gives a SEA POS a Coty-class credibility card that 100 individual testimonials can't replace.
- **UGC-style remains the dominant Meta ad format — but the proof point is now "longest active lifespan."** Across vertical spy-tool databases in 2026, UGC-style ads are the format dominating "ads with the longest active lifespans" — not just the highest CTR snapshots. The hook-in-3-seconds + first-person + use-case-specific + free-trial-mechanic combination is now the default for SaaS and POS performance creative. The brands losing on Meta are still running studio-shot, third-person, feature-list creative.
- **Screen-recording demo ads as the format SEA SaaS isn't using.** Loom, Vercel, Supabase, Linear are running 20–45-second screen-recording ad cuts that bypass the "tell me about your product" skepticism. SEA POS isn't running this format yet at all. A "60 seconds of Beep doing the lunch rush" screen-recording ad is a format wedge that's available, fast to ship, and aesthetically differentiated from every UGC-style competitor ad.

## Creative briefs for Pencil.dev

Five concepts derived from this run. None duplicates briefs from `2026-05-22-ad-inspiration.md`.

### Brief 1 — "POS that runs the back office, not just the campaign" — proactive-AI counter-positioning

- **Audience:** SEA F&B and retail operators currently on a non-AI POS (Loyverse, Slurp!, BigPOS, Xilnex) or on a reactive-AI POS (Lightspeed Restaurant, Toast IQ baseline) who are watching Square's Managerbot rollout in U.S. press and asking "where's the SEA version?"
- **Format:** A 45-second hero film + 5-slide LinkedIn carousel + Meta single-image variant. Hero film structure: open on an operator's morning routine (count cash, check stock, do schedule), cut to the same operator scrolling through proactive prompt cards on a tablet — "you're about to run out of nasi lemak rice this weekend," "Aisyah is double-booked Friday night," "Last Tuesday's lunch was slow — run a 15% lunch promo?" — each card approved with one tap.
- **Hook:** "An AI that runs the back office. Not just the ad campaign."
- **Visual direction:** Real SEA operator on camera — kopitiam, mamak, salon, or kiosk. Tablet UI cut-ins showing the proactive prompts in Bahasa Malaysia + English. End frame on the seller-approval mechanic: "You approve. The AI runs." StoreHub orange (#ff9419) on black (#2f2922), Barlow + Open Sans.
- **CTA:** "Cuba AI yang faham operasi harian. Free dengan StoreHub."
- **Differentiator vs. Square Managerbot:** Square is U.S.-only, English-only, beta-only-until-June. StoreHub can ship the SEA-language, GA-on-day-one, bank-rail-aware version first. The "seller approves" mechanic is the trust frame Square is using — borrow it directly.

### Brief 2 — "World's #1 Gen AI marketing platform. Now your SEA chain's creative engine." — Coty-credibility ad

- **Audience:** SEA mid-market F&B and retail marketing leads at chains (50+ outlets) who can't justify a Pencil price tag on a single-brand basis but would unlock the budget for a multi-outlet, multi-brand creative-OS deployment.
- **Format:** A 30-second hero film + 4-slide LinkedIn carousel + a static Meta image. Hero film opens on the Coty brands lock-up (CoverGirl, Rimmel, Sally Hansen, Max Factor) and the verbatim Coty stat lines — "10 million ads since 2018, $4 billion in media spend through the platform." Then a hard cut: "Coty's creative engine. Now built for SEA F&B and retail chains."
- **Hook:** "The world's #1 Gen AI marketing platform. Now your SEA chain's creative engine."
- **Visual direction:** Stack the Coty brand logos as the credibility opener, then cut to SEA chain footage — Toast Box, Old Town, Secret Recipe, Tealive, Boost Juice — overlaid with Pencil editor UI captures showing the same multi-brand template logic. Pencil white-on-yellow throughout.
- **CTA:** "Book a 30-minute walkthrough. See your chain's creative engine."
- **Differentiator vs. an unbranded Pencil pitch:** The Coty co-brand is the credibility card — it does the heavy lifting a generic "AI creative tool" pitch can't. Pencil owns these stat lines now; use them verbatim until the next enterprise deal lands.

### Brief 3 — "Maybank-eligible. Beep-powered." — SEA bank-credit alliance ad

- **Audience:** Malaysian SMEs operating on Beep / StoreHub who need working capital and don't realize their POS volume is the underwriting signal a SEA bank would accept.
- **Format:** A 15-second vertical hook film + 4-slide LinkedIn carousel + a static MY-Bahasa Meta image. Hero stat in the open: "Run RM10k/month on Beep. Unlock a Maybank SME line. No paperwork."
- **Hook:** "Spend on Beep. Maybank looks at your POS. The credit line opens."
- **Visual direction:** Counter-level shot of a Beep tablet showing monthly volume, cut to a Maybank SME loan offer card materializing on the same screen. Pulau Redang, Penang, or Cameron Highlands merchant B-roll. End frame on the Beep + Maybank co-brand and the credit-line number.
- **CTA:** "Tukar ke Beep. Buka kredit Maybank."
- **Differentiator vs. EasyStore × Alliance Bank:** EasyStore signed the four-way MoU with a 1,000-SME target; StoreHub can ship the merchant-facing creative version of the same structural move with a bigger bank (Maybank, CIMB, Public Bank) and a sharper merchant-side promise. The alliance is the substrate; the creative is what closes the merchant.

### Brief 4 — "Asia's 50 Best. Powered by StoreHub." — fine-dining halo + SMB relief, dual-register campaign

- **Audience:** Two parallel audiences — fine-dining operators at the top of the SEA F&B pyramid (Asia's 50 Best contenders and their kitchens) and SMB operators at the base.
- **Format:** A two-asset campaign run in parallel. Asset 1: a 60-second hero film co-cut with a single Asia's 50 Best winner kitchen — chef portraiture, plating, kitchen B-roll — closing on the StoreHub logo. Asset 2: an SMB-facing 30-second cut that opens on a hawker stall or kedai runcit operator and uses the same closing card. The two cuts share a hero line: "From the corner shop to the region's 50 Best. StoreHub powers both."
- **Hook (top):** "Powering one of Asia's 50 Best Restaurants. Powering 10,000+ corner shops."
- **Hook (SMB):** "Same POS as the 50 Best kitchens. Built for your stall."
- **Visual direction:** Two distinct registers — top cut uses cool studio lighting, slow motion, glassware close-ups; SMB cut uses warm natural light, real operator portraiture, hand-held cinematography. Same logo card closes both. StoreHub orange / black.
- **CTA (top):** "Read the {chain name} story."
- **CTA (SMB):** "Sertai 10,000+ kedai."
- **Differentiator vs. Foodics' MENA 50 Best:** Foodics owns the MENA awards halo. StoreHub can run the Asia equivalent — Asia's 50 Best is regional, accessible, and a credibility card no other SEA POS has claimed. Run both registers in the same window so the brand stretches from the kedai to the kitchen.

### Brief 5 — "60 seconds of Beep doing the lunch rush" — screen-recording demo cut

- **Audience:** Operator who has seen enough UGC-style competitor ads — wants to see the actual product working, not a talking-head testimonial.
- **Format:** A 60-second pure screen-recording ad — no voiceover artist, no actor, no stock footage. Real Beep / StoreHub tablet capture of a real lunch service, with the on-screen events captioned in plain text: "12:14 — order in," "12:14 — table 4 sends modifications," "12:15 — kitchen ticket auto-routes to halal station," "12:17 — DuitNow QR scan, payment confirmed," "12:18 — receipt printed, table cleared." Subtle background ambient (no music) — just the product doing the thing.
- **Hook:** "60 seconds of Beep. One real lunch rush."
- **Visual direction:** Screen-recorded UI throughout. Captions in Open Sans, English with Bahasa Malaysia subtitle option. End frame plain: "This is what your lunch rush looks like on Beep." StoreHub orange logo bottom-right corner only.
- **CTA:** "Lihat satu lagi minit. Cuba Beep percuma 14 hari."
- **Differentiator vs. UGC-style competitor ads:** UGC dominates Meta in 2026 — which means everyone runs the same first-person testimonial format. A pure screen-recording ad is the format wedge Loom, Vercel, Supabase, and Linear are using to bypass the "tell me about your product" skepticism. No SEA POS is running this format. Available immediately, low production cost, structurally differentiated.
