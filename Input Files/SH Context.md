## 1. Who StoreHub Is

StoreHub is a Malaysian-founded, all-in-one cloud-based business platform built for retailers and restaurants across Southeast Asia. Launched in 2013, it now serves **20,000+ retail and F&B outlets** across Malaysia, Thailand, Philippines, and beyond.

The mission is not just to build good software — it's to **democratise technology for every business, big or small**, including those who have traditionally been priced out of the technology race. StoreHub believes that in a fast-changing world, technology is not a luxury — it's what separates businesses that survive from businesses that grow.

**Positioning:** StoreHub is more than a POS system. It is a complete business operating system — a single ecosystem that replaces the fragmented stack of tools most SME owners are duct-taping together.

**The one-line pitch:** *"More Than Just a POS System."*

## 3. Core Products

### POS System (Core)
A cloud-based point-of-sale system with a front-end cash register and a cloud BackOffice. Compatible with Android and iOS (iPad Gen 7+). The backbone of the entire platform.

**What it does for the merchant:**
- Process sales fast, with or without internet (offline mode)
- Manage menus, pricing, and promotions centrally
- View real-time sales from anywhere — not just behind the counter
- Works across multiple locations from a single dashboard

**Why it matters emotionally:** *Business owners spend too many hours behind the counter. StoreHub gives them their life back — they can check the lunch rush from a supplier meeting or a school run.*

---

### BackOffice
The cloud management dashboard that connects everything. Merchants use it to manage products, view reports, configure POS layouts, manage staff, run loyalty campaigns, handle online orders, and more.

**Key capabilities:**
- Add/edit products and menus
- View sales reports and analytics by location, time, and item
- Manage stock levels and supplier orders
- Configure loyalty programme and Engage campaigns
- Manage employees and clock-in records
- Handle QR Order & Pay and Beep Delivery orders

---

### QR Order & Pay
Customers scan a QR code at their table, counter, or entrance — browse the digital menu, order, and pay directly from their phone. No app download required.

**Payment methods supported (Malaysia):** GrabPay, Touch 'n Go, credit/debit cards, FPX (online banking), Apple Pay

**Modes:**
- **Pay at Counter** — customer orders via QR, pays at cashier
- **Pay Later** — customer orders and pays after meal

**Why it matters:** Reduces order errors from verbal communication. Frees up staff. Speeds up table turnover. Works at peak hours without needing more staff.

---

### Beep (Delivery & Online Ordering)
StoreHub's own delivery and online ordering platform. Merchants launch their own food delivery without paying 20–35% commission to GrabFood or Foodpanda.

**Features:**
- Own-branded ordering page
- Manage delivery zones and pricing
- Takeaway and self-pickup options
- Orders reflected in POS and StoreHub Manager app
- Beep QR Order (dine-in) and Beep Delivery (takeaway/delivery) as separate channels

**The emotional hook:** *"You're paying how much in delivery fees? Keep 100% of every order."*

---

### Engage (SMS Marketing Automation)
Automated SMS marketing powered by customer transaction data. No manual work — it runs itself.

**Campaign types:**
- Welcome new customers
- Cashback reminder (nudge customers to return and redeem)
- Expiring cashback alert (urgency-driven win-back)
- Win back lost customers (re-engage lapsed customers automatically)
- Birthday promotions
- Boost Google reviews (prompt happy customers post-purchase)
- Custom one-time SMS blasts for flash sales or promotions

**Why it matters:** Most SME owners know they should be doing customer marketing. None of them have the time. Engage does it automatically from day one.

---

### Loyalty / Membership (CRM)
Built-in customer loyalty programme with cashback, points, tiered membership, birthday rewards, and free gifts. No separate app needed for customers — works through the POS.

**Core features:**
- Customer database (name, phone, email, total spend, cashback balance, last visit)
- Cashback settings (percentage, daily limits, expiry)
- Tiered membership levels (Bronze, Silver, Gold)
- Points-based rewards
- Cashback summary dashboard with retention metrics
- CSV import/export for customer data

**Why cashback beats punch cards:** Instant, digital, unforgeable, and it creates a financial reason to return — customers come back to *use* what they've earned.

---

### Marketplace Integration (Malaysia only)
Powered by Sellercraft. Syncs online orders from Shopee, Lazada, TikTok Shop, Zalora, WooCommerce, Shopify, and Magento directly into the StoreHub POS and BackOffice. Stock levels update automatically after every transaction across all channels.

**Why it matters:** Merchants selling across multiple platforms waste hours reconciling inventory manually. This eliminates that entirely.

## 8. Ad Types & Copy Guidelines

### Promo Ads
**Format:** Video or static
**Purpose:** Drive direct response — lead capture (book a demo)
**What the ad shows:** The current discount or price anchor (e.g., 55% off, RM3.40/day) + a POS terminal so the viewer can visually connect it to StoreHub
**Copy style:** Bold, direct, urgent. Lead with the offer. Support with a clear product visual. No waffle.

**Example hooks:**
- "55% Off StoreHub — Today Only"
- "RM3.40/day to run your entire restaurant"
- "Switch to the #1 POS System in Malaysia — Now at 55% Off"

---

### BTC (Behind The Counter) Videos
**Format:** Documentary/podcast video clips
**Purpose:** Build trust through authentic merchant stories; warm audiences and drive demo bookings
**Structure:** StoreHub podcast host interviews a real merchant about their journey, their struggles, and how StoreHub helped
**Copy style:** Emotionally driven, story-first. Lead with the merchant's reality — the grind, the turning point, the win. The product is the supporting character, not the hero.

**Example hooks:**
- "He almost closed his cafe. Then he found one system that changed everything."
- "She runs 3 outlets — alone. Here's how."
- "From chaos at peak hour to running like clockwork."

---

### VP (Value Proposition) Videos
**Format:** Short-form product demo or feature highlight video
**Purpose:** Educate on a specific feature or benefit; drive demo bookings
**Copy style:** Clear, benefit-led. One feature per ad. Show the problem it solves before showing the feature.

**Example hooks:**
- "Still reconciling inventory by hand? There's a better way."
- "Your customers want to order from their phones. Make it easy."
- "Your competitor is already using this. Are you?"

## 10. Copywriting Philosophy

### The Merchant is the Hero
StoreHub ads are written for real business owners — not generic entrepreneurs. Every piece of copy should reflect their specific reality: the double-shift, the missed lunch, the staff who called in sick during peak hour, the end-of-month reconciliation that takes all night.

**Speak to the grind before you sell the solution.**

### Emotional Drivers (in order of strength)
1. **Fear of falling behind** — competitors are already modernising; every day without the right tools is lost ground
2. **Relief from chaos** — the promise of "it just works" is deeply compelling to operators drowning in operational friction
3. **Pride in growth** — merchants want to build something. Position StoreHub as the infrastructure that makes growth possible.
4. **Financial pain** — delivery platform commissions, cash leakage, overstocking — these are real, quantifiable losses that cut into margins daily

### Copy Rules
- **Lead with the merchant's pain, not the product's features**
- **Be specific** — "RM3.40/day" beats "affordable pricing"; "45 minutes of end-of-day reconciliation" beats "saves time"
- **One idea per ad** — never stack two hooks or two products
- **English for Win%** (MY), Chinese for SQL%, Malay for volume — match language to objective
- **Questions create self-audit** — "How much are you paying in delivery fees?" makes the merchant calculate their own loss before the ad says a word
- **Never be corporate** — no jargon, no formal register; write like a trusted peer who runs a business themselves

### Tone by Ad Type
| Ad Type | Tone |
|---|---|
| Promo | Bold, urgent, direct — let the number do the work |
| BTC / Merchant Story | Warm, authentic, empathetic — earn trust before asking for anything |
| VP / Feature | Clear, practical, solution-focused — one problem, one answer |

## 13. PRIME DIRECTIVE — The 50/50 Creative Rule

> **At least 50% of every ad batch must be Creative Wildcards — highly creative, hooky, or deliberately absurd.**

StoreHub uses generative AI tools (Pencil.dev, Nano Banana, Veo 3) to unlock creative possibilities that traditional production cannot. This is not a nice-to-have. It is a structural requirement of every iteration.

### Why This Exists
The performance data shows the market has been trained on a narrow range of formats: split-screen hardware, promo price banners, unboxing videos. These work — but they fatigue. The next wave of performance comes from creative that breaks the pattern entirely. When the merchant is scrolling at 11pm after a double shift, they will not stop for another POS ad. They will stop for something they've never seen before.

### The 50/50 Split

**Safe Half (50%) — Exploit & Optimise**
These are rooted in proven patterns from the performance data:
- Choose Your Fighter comparison
- BAU / seasonal promo with hardware
- F&B vertical-specific messaging
- Split-screen price anchor
- Question-hook (profit/delivery fee)

**Wildcard Half (50%) — Creative Chaos**
These push boundaries. They are unexpected. Some will fail. Some will be the best-performing ads ever made. Every batch must include:
- At minimum 2 concepts that would make a traditional marketer nervous
- At minimum 1 concept using absurdist humour or surreal visual storytelling
- At minimum 1 concept that leads with pure entertainment before revealing StoreHub

---

### Wildcard Creative Framework

#### Category 1 — Absurdist Problem Exaggeration
Take a real merchant pain and dramatise it to an absurd extreme. The humour makes the insight land harder.

**Examples:**
- A restaurant owner with 47 tablets on the counter, each showing a different app, visibly sweating — "There's a better way."
- Someone doing end-of-day reconciliation while wearing a full winter jacket because it's 3am and they've been there since 6am — "StoreHub closes in 5 minutes. Not 5 hours."
- A merchant manually counting inventory with an abacus in a futuristic city — "You're running a 2025 business with 1995 tools."
- A waiter sprinting between tables, dropping plates, taking orders on paper, screaming — slow zoom out to reveal the whole restaurant is on fire. "Peak hour without StoreHub."

#### Category 2 — Unexpected Visual Metaphors
The product is not shown literally. Instead, an unexpected metaphor makes the benefit viscerally clear.

**Examples:**
- A merchant carrying a mountain on their back, walking up stairs — then they pick up a StoreHub device and the mountain evaporates.
- A restaurant shown as a spinning plate act — the owner desperately keeping 8 plates spinning. One by one they crash... except one. The plate with the StoreHub logo spins perfectly.
- Split screen: left side = spaghetti of cables, tablets, paper, stress / right side = single clean screen, calm merchant.
- The merchant's brain shown as a filing cabinet that keeps exploding open — vs a brain that looks like a clean dashboard.

#### Category 3 — Cultural / Meme-Native Hooks
Ads that speak the native language of social media — aware of trends, self-referential, funny, or designed to be saved/shared.

**Examples:**
- "POV: Your competitor just switched to StoreHub" — merchant celebrating while their rival is drowning in paperwork
- Fake job listing: "Hiring: Superhuman who can manage POS, inventory, staff schedules, delivery orders, loyalty rewards, AND make coffee. OR... just use StoreHub."
- Text-only ad that reads like a WhatsApp message from a stressed restaurant owner — then pivots to the solution
- "Things that should not exist in 2025: [list of manual processes the merchant still does]"

#### Category 4 — Emotional Gut-Punch
No humour. Pure raw truth. A quiet, unadorned moment that lands because it's real.

**Examples:**
- A merchant alone in their empty restaurant at 2am doing paperwork. No music. No VO. Just the sound of a calculator clicking. Text appears: "You didn't open a restaurant to do this." — "StoreHub. Book a free demo."
- Slow-motion shot of a child knocking on a closed restaurant door — their parent inside, head down, managing stock. "The business was always the priority. Until StoreHub made it easier to choose both."
- Time-lapse of a restaurant from empty to full to empty again — the owner visible the entire time, never leaving. "20,000 merchants finally got their time back."

#### Category 5 — Product as Hero (Unexpected POV)
The POS system or feature becomes a character. Shot from an unusual perspective.

**Examples:**
- POV from the POS screen — watching the first order of the day come in, then the rush, then quiet. Text: "We see everything you do. We make it easier."
- The Falcon 1 hardware introduced like a luxury car reveal — full cinematic treatment, dramatic lighting, music drop — "The most powerful tool in your restaurant. And it fits on your counter."
- Stop-motion where business chaos (paper, pens, receipts) slowly assembles itself into a clean StoreHub terminal

---

### Wildcard Rules
1. **The brand must still be identifiable** — wildcard does not mean unbranded. Logo, colour, and CTA remain.
2. **The hook must earn attention in the first 1–2 seconds** — if it doesn't stop the scroll, it failed.
3. **It must still drive to "Book a free demo"** — creative chaos with a clear conversion intent.
4. **Never be weird for weird's sake** — every absurd idea must connect back to a real merchant pain or StoreHub benefit. The twist should make the insight *more* clear, not less.
5. **Fail fast, learn fast** — wildcards that miss are expected. The point is to find the one that breaks out. Document learnings in the knowledge base either way.

---

### Wildcard Scoring (for results analysis)
When evaluating wildcard ads, score on two axes:
- **Scroll-stop score**: CTR + Hook Rate vs benchmark
- **Intent score**: SQL% + CPWon vs benchmark

A wildcard that wins on both = **promote to core rotation**
A wildcard that wins scroll-stop only = **iterate the concept with stronger offer**
A wildcard that wins neither = **retire and document why**
